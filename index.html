The front moved

Twenty years ago the front of a plumbing shop was a sign on Boulder Highway and a half-page in the phone book. Now it's a listing that gets checked before anyone sees a truck — hours, photos, reviews, ads — while the crews are still out.

Each piece looks small. Together they decide whether you get considered when somebody needs a plumber right now.

The desk — what gets done every week

One person keeps the front. After the trucks are in.

Frontage is one desk — a person you can text — working the three Google products this valley uses to find a plumber, every week, in a set order. Most weeks, a yes or a no is the only thing we need from you.

The listing.

Hours that match how you actually run — including the after-hours line, if you answer it. Services written the way people search here: water heater, tankless flush, drain, repipe, slab leak, gas line. This month's job photos, captioned. A post a week. Questions answered. Your license number where Nevada wants it. This is the page a stranger reads before they decide you're real.

The reviews.

Every review gets a reply that sounds like a real shop, not a template — the five-star and the one-star. A bad review with a calm answer under it reads better than a good one with nothing. We draft. You approve the touchy ones. We never write, buy, or fake a review.

The ads. The Local Services box.

Local Services is the pay-per-lead box at the top of a Google search; Search ads sit under it. Both run inside a written monthly cap you choose and fund on your own card. Junk search terms shut off. Every Local Services lead rated the day it comes in, with the right reason — the dispute button is gone, so the rating is the lever that's left. Job types and service area tightened so you're not billed for calls from places you don't serve.

Most agencies bill a percentage of ad spend — the range agencies themselves publish is 10 to 20 percent — so the agency earns more when you spend more. Ours is $1,800 whether your cap is $1,000 or $6,000. That's not a discount; it's a different incentive. When we say "spend less on that," it doesn't come out of our pocket, so you get the honest version.

Why ninety

Setup takes weeks — access, tracking, job types, the first pages — and July tests a water heater and a marketing contract the same way. So we ask for one season. After that you shouldn't be trapped, so you aren't.
